# Content Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the content strategy for the Moving in Mobile website.

It explains:

- the role of editorial content
- how content supports lead generation
- how content supports SEO and GEO
- how neighborhood pages should be developed
- how relocation content should be structured
- how The Rowe Report should be reused
- how buyer and seller content should evolve
- how IDX content should complement original content
- how content should be reviewed, updated, and measured

The goal is to make Moving in Mobile a useful local real-estate resource rather than simply a collection of marketing pages.

---

# Content Strategy Objective

The site should help users make better real-estate and relocation decisions.

Content should answer questions such as:

- What is it like to live in this area?
- How do nearby communities compare?
- What should I know before moving?
- What should buyers expect?
- What should sellers prepare for?
- What homes are currently available?
- What local issues should homeowners understand?
- How can Tina help?

The content should build trust before asking for contact information.

---

# Core Content Principles

Moving in Mobile content should be:

- useful
- locally specific
- accurate
- current where necessary
- easy to understand
- professionally written
- conversational
- non-salesy
- original
- compliant with real-estate advertising and Fair Housing requirements

Avoid content that exists only to create another indexable URL.

---

# Brand Voice

The preferred tone is:

- knowledgeable
- friendly
- calm
- professional
- local
- practical
- confident without being pushy

The site should sound like a trusted advisor explaining real-world choices.

Preferred language includes:

- "Here is what to consider..."
- "One tradeoff is..."
- "Buyers often ask..."
- "This may be worth considering if..."
- "What I often see is..."
- "The answer depends on what matters most to you..."

Avoid exaggerated marketing language.

---

# Content and Lead Generation

Content should support lead generation naturally.

Preferred sequence:

    User has a question
            |
            v
    Site provides a useful answer
            |
            v
    User gains confidence in Tina
            |
            v
    Site offers a relevant next action
            |
            v
    User searches, saves, asks, or contacts

The content should create reasons to contact Tina rather than simply demanding contact information.

---

# Content and SEO

Strong content supports SEO through:

- unique page topics
- local relevance
- useful headings
- internal linking
- route-specific titles
- search intent alignment
- content depth
- original expertise

SEO should help users discover useful content.

It should not dictate awkward writing.

---

# Content and GEO

Generative Engine Optimization should influence content structure.

Important GEO traits include:

- direct answers
- natural-language headings
- specific geographic references
- original local insight
- comparison content
- structured facts
- clear relationships between pages
- updated information
- strong entity consistency

The site should answer real questions in a way that search engines and generative systems can understand.

A strong neighborhood page may include:

    H1

    Location
    Where Fairhope sits relative to Mobile and Baldwin County

    Lifestyle
    Downtown, bayfront, restaurants, parks, events

# Fair Housing Requirements

All neighborhood and relocation content must comply with Fair Housing requirements.

Avoid language suggesting who should or should not live in an area based on protected characteristics.

Do not describe areas using demographic steering.

Avoid language such as:

- family neighborhood
- young professional area
- safe community
- good schools as a subjective guarantee
- Christian community
- retirement neighborhood unless formally designated and legally appropriate

Use objective property and location characteristics.

---

# School Content

School-related information should be factual and neutral.

Appropriate content may include:

- district names
- official attendance-zone resources
- links to school-system websites

Avoid subjective claims such as:

- best schools
- top families choose this area
- better student population

Encourage buyers to independently evaluate schools based on their priorities.

---

# Safety and Crime

Do not describe neighborhoods as:

- safe
- unsafe
- low crime
- high crime

without authoritative context.

A better approach is to direct users to official public data.

Tina should not make subjective safety judgments for clients or website visitors.

---

# Avoid Content Cannibalization

Do not publish multiple pages that answer essentially the same question without a clear distinction.

Example risk:

    /fairhope
    /living-in-fairhope
    /moving-to-fairhope
    /fairhope-alabama-real-estate

If these pages contain largely the same content, they may compete with one another.

Prefer one strong page or clearly differentiated intent.

---

# Content Consolidation

If multiple old pages overlap heavily:

1. determine the strongest page
2. merge useful content
3. redirect obsolete route where appropriate
4. update internal links
5. update sitemap
6. review Search Console

Do not leave duplicate pages indefinitely.

---

# Content Removal

When removing a public page:

- determine whether it has traffic
- determine whether backlinks exist
- redirect to a relevant alternative where appropriate
- remove from sitemap
- update internal links

Do not simply delete established URLs without considering SEO implications.

# IDX Content Boundaries

Do not manually copy IDX Broker pages into React content.

Maintain a clear separation:

    React editorial site:
    expertise, context, guidance, brand

    IDX:
    live MLS inventory, search, listing detail

This separation improves maintainability and keeps responsibilities clear.

---

# Content Before IDX

The current documentation and architecture work should finish before broad new content expansion.

IDX should be implemented first because:

- future content CTAs need stable property-search URLs
- neighborhood pages should connect to real searches
- search paths should be known before content architecture expands

This reduces rework.

- editorial content and IDX should remain complementary rather than duplicative;
- Fair Housing, factual accuracy, and trusted-advisor tone apply to all published content;
- broad content expansion should follow the current IDX infrastructure milestone.
